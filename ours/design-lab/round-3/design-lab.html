What does a brand built to last 50 years look like?

Ten new directions, plus your favourites from rounds 1 and 2 rebuilt on the same board, so every design is judged on exactly the same pieces: mark, colours, type, homepage, the app's approval screen, a customer graphic, the moment of saying yes, and the voice. Next, Codex tests them with our customer personas, round after round, until one wins consistently.

What has already lasted decades

Systems, human touch, clarity and ritual.

British road signs have stayed readable for 60 years. Postage stamps have been around since 1840. Matisse's paper cut-outs still feel fresh 70 years on, and a keyboard key is understood by everyone. None of them is just a logo. Each one is a system people trust, with a human feel and a ritual attached.

What changes after AI

Content becomes endless. Taste and trust become rare.

AI makes posts cheap, so what's scarce is taste, trust and the owner's own "yes". Computers are becoming things you talk to and that fit around your day. Brands become systems that adapt to each customer. So every direction here answers two questions: how does saying yes feel, and how does the customer's own brand shine?

  • Systems, not logosAn identity that adapts to every customer.
  • The human yesApproval is the product. Make it feel like a signature.
  • Rhythm and ritualA week at a time, at the right time of day.
  • Clarity outlasts fashionDesigns that still read in 2076.
  • Joy is a featureCreative people buy tools that feel creative.
How we'll find the winner

Blind rounds with three customer personas, until one design wins every time.

The test pack

One image of each board, plus a PDF of all of them, saved in the repo and in project files. Codex scores each board as each persona, in a shuffled order every round.

What gets scored

  1. Stops me scrolling (memorable)
  2. Feels like it's for a business like mine
  3. Worth £79 to £299 a month
  4. Fun and creative
  5. I'd know what to do on the app screen
  6. Will still look good in 10 years

A fair warning

AI personas are a fast filter, not real customers. Once a winner is consistent, I'd show the top two to three real business owners before we build it.
